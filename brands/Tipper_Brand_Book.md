## 1. Brand Strategy

### Who We Are
Tipper is an omnichannel community engagement platform that connects users to their respective networks in society through entities and events they engage with on a regular basis.

Three pillars: **People + Places + Events**

### Our Purpose
> To reshape our interactions in everyday society

### Our Mission
1. Establishing online and real-world accountability for user actions via blockchain
2. Becoming a census of verification for users
3. Orchestrating a seamless data-driven personalized platform
4. Revitalizing a cultural flow to shape a favorable future for society

### Our Vision
> A macrocosm within your reach

### Our Values
- **Synergy** — Collective, Friendly, Interdependent
- **Candor** — Plain-spoken, Authentic, Trustworthy
- **Inclusivity** — Engaging, Accessible, Bespoke
- **Ubiquity** — Omnipresent, Bold, Giving

### Our Tagline
> **"One tip at a time"**

Taps into our core purpose, encapsulating the collective ethos of the Tipper community that strives to make society a better place, one tip at a time.

### Target Audience
The Tipper community welcomes a broad range of individuals from different backgrounds, with various interests and passions. Focus revolves around people driven by the desire to engage with each other and contribute to their community.

Two groups: **Entities** and **Users**

## 2. Logo System

### Logo (p.22)
The Tipper logo is the most significant aspect of the brand. It embodies what the brand stands for and acts as a signature across all branded media. Not every visual issue can be solved with the logo alone — it should be wielded sensibly and in line with these guidelines.

### Logo Anatomy: Brand Mark (p.23)
The brand mark represents the letter "T" of "Tipper" in the form of a **speech bubble**, symbolizing interaction and connection, and embodying abstract representations of the three pillars:
- **People:** Tag
- **Places:** Location Pin
- **Events:** Conversations (Speech Bubble)

### Logo Anatomy: The Ellipsis (p.24)
The three dots within the brand mark are called the **Ellipsis**; an individual dot is an **Ellipse**. They sit at the center of the brand mark and represent the microcosms within the macrocosm that is Tipper. (Associated metaphors: road, domino, sticky notes, abstract face, loading speech, the three pillars, the three industries, breadcrumb pathway, individuals of a community, microcosms within macrocosm.)

### Logo Anatomy: Typeface (p.25)
- Typeface: **Fredoka** (Custom weight 590, width 102)
- The rounded typeface conveys a friendly and welcoming mood; it's a quirky and unique variable typeface
- The dot of the letter "i" is integrated into the brand mark
- Conveys fluidity, friendliness, and connectivity with a sharp, modern, minimal, clever design

### Logo Construction (p.26)
The logo is crafted using absolute proportions (based on the golden ratio), with its focal point on the brand mark. Changing the slightest detail will alter its balance and must be avoided at all cost.

### Logo Clear Space (p.27)
A minimum amount of clear space — the height of the letter "e" — must surround the entire logo at all times. This space can be larger than indicated, but never smaller.

### Alternate Logo (p.28)
A version of the logo includes the tagline **"one tip at a time"** to highlight brand purpose. This is the version of choice for any communication application. The tagline can never be modified and must always follow this lockup structure.

### Alternate Logo Clear Space (p.29)
Follows the same spacing rules as the primary logo. The tagline sits at equal spacing from its surroundings, at the size of an Ellipse.

### Brand Mark (Standalone) (p.30)
The brand mark can be used standalone (without wordmark) when the full logo lockup is too big to fit — e.g., social media or app icons.

### Brand Mark Clear Space (p.31)
Same minimum clear space system as the primary logo: at least the height of the letter "e" around the entire brand mark.

### Minimum Sizes (p.32)
- Main logo: never smaller than **95px** wide
- Secondary (alternate) logo: never smaller than **122px** wide
- Brand mark: never smaller than **36px** wide

### Logo B&W (p.33)
Black and white are not official brand colors and must be avoided by default, but can be used when media can only display grayscale/B&W. The white version of the logo is specially tweaked (bolder) for contrast on black backgrounds — always use the designated white version.

### Logo Colors (p.34–36)
- **Ruby** — Primary brand color; most dominant. Displayed solely in its shade or on Diamond background. Never reversed. Pertains to **Food and Beverage**.
- **Amethyst** — Secondary brand color. Displayed solely in its shade or on Diamond background. Never reversed. Pertains to **Non-Profit Organization**.
- **Emerald** — Secondary brand color. Displayed solely in its shade or on Diamond background. Never reversed. Pertains to **Creative** industry.

### Logo Misuse (p.37)
Do NOT:
- Apply effects
- Add a stroke
- Color the dots
- Use multiple colors
- Use unofficial colors
- Fill with visual/texture
- Stretch or skew
- Rotate
- Change kerning

## 3. Typography

### Typeface (p.39)
- **Work Sans** — primary typeface (based loosely on early Grotesques). Regular weights optimized for on-screen text at 14–48px, also usable in print. Extreme weights designed for display use (web and print). Free download at Google Fonts. Weights: Thin, ExtraLight, Light, Regular, Medium, SemiBold, Bold, ExtraBold, Black.
- **Arial** — fallback typeface for situations where Work Sans may not be installed (e.g., shared Word/PowerPoint docs, email communications).

### Typographic Commandments (p.40)
- **Left aligned, ragged right** — align left and rag right for readability; avoid rivers, orphans, widows
- **Let's embrace sentence case** — capitalize only the first letter of the first word
- **Tetris' the name, alignment's the game** — align baselines/x-heights; use a master grid for advanced layouts
- **When in trouble, make it double** — use contrast (size/weight) to create hierarchy
- **When too long, say so long** — never alter kerning/tracking (set to 0) of Work Sans or Arial
- **Stretched is far-fetched** — body copy: 50–70 characters per line; titles: no more than 3–4 words per line

### Type Misuse (p.41)
Do NOT:
- Apply effects
- Use all caps
- Use Work Sans and Arial together
- Use italic fonts
- Use unapproved color
- Use unapproved typeface
- Use too many weights
- Rotate
- Use bold weights for body copy

## 4. Color Palette

### Color Swatches (p.43)

Tipper is a multicolored brand with **Ruby** as dominant. The palette is made up of three main colors, each with a tint and shade, plus three neutrals (Pearl, Diamond, Obsidian) typically used for backgrounds.

| Color | Association | Traits |
|---|---|---|
| **Ruby** | Food and Beverage | Dynamic, fun, uplifting |
| **Amethyst** | Non-Profit Organization | Trustworthy, sensitive, inspiring |
| **Emerald** | Creatives | Prosperous, revitalizing, insightful |

**Ruby**
- Tint: `#ffdede` (RGB 255,222,222 / CMYK 0,15,6,0)
- Base: `#ff5c5c` (RGB 255,88,93 / CMYK 0,78,57,0)
- Shade: `#691c32` (RGB 110,0,51 / CMYK 37,100,58,42)

**Amethyst**
- Tint: `#ece3ff` (RGB 236,227,255 / CMYK 6,10,0,0)
- Base: `#9e75ff` (RGB 158,117,255 / CMYK 51,58,0,0)
- Shade: `#362c77` (RGB 54,44,119 / CMYK 97,100,19,7)

**Emerald**
- Tint: `#d5f3ef` (RGB 213,243,239 / CMYK 15,0,7,0)
- Base: `#2cc4ae` (RGB 44,196,174 / CMYK 67,0,41,0)
- Shade: `#003947` (RGB 0,57,71 / CMYK 96,65,52,45)

**Neutrals**
- Pearl: `#fff2e5` (RGB 255,242,229 / CMYK 0,4,8,0)
- Diamond: `#ffffff` (RGB 255,255,255 / CMYK 0,0,0,0)
- Obsidian: `#151828` (RGB 21,24,40 / CMYK 85,77,54,70)

> Reproducing colors accurately is essential for Tipper's brand identity to appear consistent across all content. Tints and shades support the two primary/secondary colors and should be used proportionately.

### Color Ratio (p.44)
A guide chart balances base and accent colors across visual communication — tints and shades of primary/secondary colors should complement the base color proportionately whenever required.

### Color Misuse (p.45)
Do NOT:
- Use HEX #000000 as black
- Use colors as a gradient
- Use unapproved colors
- Lower color opacity
- Use wrong color combinations
- Use light colors on Pearl (poor contrast)

## 5. Photography

### Photography Tiers (p.47)
Photography is divided into three tiers celebrating different inspiring moments across the industries:
1. **Lifestyle**
2. **Activity**
3. **Event**

Photos should be carefully styled and curated to capture the essence of the subject — person, activity, or event.

### Imagery Example (p.48)
Photography aims to capture the Tipper community in its greatest moments. Ideal visuals combine the three pillars: people, places, events. Images should be treated at **high contrast** with a **rich, warm color tone**.

*Note: placeholder images in the book are for creative direction only and should not be used in actual campaigns.*

### Photo Misuse (p.49)
Photos ought to be genuine, upbeat, and approachable. Lighting should feel natural even when artificially lit. Subjects should be immersed in the moment, not posing or staring at the camera.

Do NOT:
- Use washed-out images
- Use staged images
- Use obscure images
- Use heavy filters
- Capture weird angles
- Show empty places

## 6. Graphic Assets

### Icon Tiers (p.51)
A set of primary icons representing the three pillars has been created to enhance the brand's fun, fluid visual system. Icon colors are interchangeable based on industry (only official primary/secondary palette colors). Icons serve as accent visuals — smaller supportive graphics in layouts, not focal elements.

### Tips & Doodles (p.52)
Doodles and hand-written notes add a personalized, human touch to designs. They're part of the graphic language, giving the impression of hand-written notes sprinkled throughout copy for the one-of-a-kind Tipper community.

## 7. Mascots — "Tipper Troop" (p.53–57)

The Tipper Troop is a family of quirky brand mascots that add wit and personality to the brand — personified versions of the brand, each representing an industry. Their initials spell **"TPR"**, pronounced like "Tipper."

### Twig — "The Caregiver" (p.55, Ruby)
Twigford "Twig" is a helpful guide and great motivator to their community. Their purpose is to understand and connect with people in meaningful ways and make a real difference in the lives of their community members. They feel the duty to give back to society and act responsibly to keep positive balance in the world.

### Pax — "The Entrepreneur" (p.56, Emerald)
Paxton "Pax" is a hands-on learner who strives to live their best life while sharing it with others in their immediate circle. They choose the path less traveled, immerse all their senses in the present moment, and are goal-oriented and self-reliant.

### Rad — "The Discoverer" (p.57, Amethyst)
Radbert "Rad" is an enthusiastic free-spirit who radiates a contagious upbeat vibe that draws people in. They seek out life's joys and pleasures, include others on the journey of discovery, and love heartfelt, emotionally connective conversations.
