# GAF Brand Design Brief
*Prepared for Claude Design*

---

## 1. Identity

**Name:** GAF; Guild of Adventurers and Frontiers

**Core concept:** GAF is "the guild of all guilds." Where a traditional guild is specific to one trade, craft, or art, GAF is the shared hall that any guild, any pursuit, any serious project can call home. It is not a school and not a generic co-working space; it is a place where people who already know what they are building come to work, gather, and question their assumptions in the company of others doing the same.

**Positioning statement (working):** GAF is where people bring their own frontier; not to be taught, but to be guided.

---

## 2. Mission & Philosophy

GAF is a physical, communal co-creation space. People use it three ways: they drop into events, they organize their own events or gatherings around their own projects, or they work there all day building something of their own.

The underlying belief driving GAF: **assumptions, not lack of effort, are what slow people down.** GAF exists to bring the wisdom and rigor behind "startup science" into the room, quietly, without the noise, adrenaline, or overwhelm that usually comes attached to it. It's a place to be questioned, redirected, and to stay dynamic; not a place to be hyped up.

**Messaging pillar** (this is the intellectual core of the brand voice, not necessarily verbatim copy):

> It's the assumptions that kill us and slow our growth.
>
> 1. What are the preconceptions you have about your life, your work, the world?
> 2. What are the facts that might not be true?
> 3. What are the language patterns you're using that frame your solutions; and quietly frame your assumptions along with them?
> 4. Are you framing your questions (business, sales, growth, acquisitions) around your ideal outcome and your real constraints?

Any tagline, headline system, or content voice should draw from this spirit of Socratic, guided self-questioning; calm and pointed, not motivational-poster energy.

---

## 3. Audience & Personas

GAF speaks to someone who does not want to search for direction; they want to sit in a place that can guide them. Three personas:

**1. The Visitor** — comes for a specific event, drops in, may not be a regular. Curious, sampling the guild before committing further.

**2. The Organizer** — books or uses the space to run an event or gathering around their own project. Needs the space to flex around their agenda while still feeling unmistakably "GAF."

**3. The Resident Builder** — comes to work all day and create their own project. The core, habitual member; the person the guild-hall atmosphere is really built for.

*(These three should inform tone and detail across every touchpoint; a wall crest speaks to all three differently than a member card would.)*

---

## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

**Feel:** Playful, but never at the expense of being taken seriously. People at GAF are serious about what they're doing; they simply use play as the method to get there, instead of "work," which implies grinding.

**Reference figure:** Sir Ernest Henry Shackleton, and specifically his "Men Wanted" advertisement; honest about hardship, cold, danger, and long odds, and still the most famous recruitment ad in history because it respected the reader enough to tell the truth. That's the emotional register GAF is after: adventurous, but never naive about risk. Nobody who signs up for a real frontier does so without knowing what it costs.

**Words and postures to avoid:**
- **"Hustle"** — implies constantly chasing something; the opposite of GAF's posture.
- **Anything "startupy"** — pitch-deck energy, growth-hacking jargon, forced excitement. GAF applies the *wisdom* behind startup science without the noise. Everything is technically a "startup" in some sense (even a small business); GAF doesn't need to say the word to honor the discipline behind it.

---

## 5. Visual Identity Direction

### Color Palette
Deep, grounded, forest-and-dusk tones; nothing bright, nothing corporate.

- **Primary:** Near-black deep forest green (reference: solid deep green swatch provided, close to a hunter/bottle green pushed almost to black)
- **Secondary:** Forest/pewter grays; the dusty sage-gray and slate-gray family (reference: "In The Garden," "Dark Pewter," "Laurel Garland," "Duck Green" style tones) to symbolize simplicity and fulfillment
- **Tertiary:** Wood brown, pulled directly from the existing space's walnut-toned furniture
- **Accent (to develop):** A warm, low-key dawn tone; "the dark green that breaks at the end of the night just before sunrise." This should read as a single moment of warmth against the cool dark palette, not a bright accent color. Claude Design should propose 2–3 candidate hexes for this and test them against the primary green.

### Typography
A brush-stroke direction closer to Katakana-style brushwork; ink-brush weight and gesture, not a literal font-conversion of Japanese script. This should be paired with a clean, readable body typeface so the brushwork stays special-occasion (headlines, the crest, event titles) rather than fighting for legibility everywhere.

This is an intentional fusion: Eastern brush calligraphy weight, layered onto a Western medieval-guild structural language (seals, crests, hall signage). The two should feel deliberately combined, not like two unrelated references competing.

### Iconography & Motifs
- Guild crest / seal as the core mark
- Compass, map, or frontier-adjacent symbolism (ties directly to "Adventurers and Frontiers")
- Ink-brush linework for supporting marks or dividers

## 6. Physical Space Application (Priority)

The physical space is the first priority for this brand, ahead of digital. Current space context, for material and layout grounding:

- A mini lounge/couch area (grey sofa, cushions) next to a dark walnut library shelf full of books
- White painted stone-brick accent walls
- A black spiral staircase leading to a mezzanine loft with wrought-iron railings and overhead wood-bracket shelving holding books and trailing plants
- A coffee/tea station and a separate water station, both on matching walnut cabinets
- One large central wooden communal table with black mesh chairs; the main working/gathering surface
- A whiteboard and corkboard for active planning
- Greenery dispersed throughout, indoor potted plants and an entrance row of terracotta pots
- A black-painted ceiling accent in the loft area

This existing material palette (walnut wood, black metal, white brick, black ceiling) already leans toward the guild-hall direction. The new GAF color and type system should layer onto this rather than fight it.

**First deliverable: the wall crest.** This is the first signage point and should be treated as the flagship mark; the piece that, on its own, tells a visitor what kind of place this is before they read a word.

**Note on the donation station:** There is an existing hand-made sign near the coffee station ("One Tip at a Time," "Work. Brew. Create.," with cartoon mascots). This function is being handed to **Tipper**, GAF's sister software product, which will manage community and donations. Claude Design does not need to solve this touchpoint now; it should simply be aware that this wall will eventually carry Tipper's branding alongside GAF's, and the two should be able to coexist without clashing.

**Suggested signage rollout order:**
1. Wall crest (flagship mark)
2. Wayfinding / zone signage (library corner, communal table, coffee & water stations)
3. Member-facing materials (cards, event signage)

## 9. The Founding Framework: WHO / WHAT / WHY / WHERE / HOW / WHEN

This is the underlying logic beneath everything above; the questions GAF used to locate itself, and a structure GAF may also offer members as a way to locate *themselves* at different stages of their own growth. Two uses, one framework:

- **Internal use:** grounds brand voice, positioning, and copy (what follows below feeds directly into Sections 4, 6, and 7 of this brief).
- **Possible member-facing use:** the same six questions, offered as a periodic check-in tool for residents and organizers to ask "where am I now, and where do I want to be"; a companion to the assumptions-questioning pillar in Section 2, not a replacement for it.

### WHO
*Get to know yourself, in the Ancient Greek sense; who is doing the work, who are "we."*

- We are the seed of a new design; GAF is our new design of society.
- We are the ones who would take the time to understand you when the world didn't.
- We are the new frontier. We are where frontiers are discovered.

**Ties to:** the "guild of all guilds" positioning (Section 1) and the Shackleton-honest tone (Section 4). This is the emotional reason someone stays past a first visit.

### WHAT
*What GAF is doing, what it is, what it restructures.*

- An opinionated approach to a suffocating society.
- A space that hosts renegades.
- Opening a new way for people to be resourceful; allowing individuals the leisure of finding their own productivity.
- Reinforcing ideas and actively bringing visions to life.
- A neutral space for creativity, exploration, and discovery.
- Clarifying the bridge between every conceptual entity and the community that helps it thrive.

**Ties to:** the anti-hustle, anti-startupy voice guardrails (Section 4). "Opinionated" and "hosts renegades" give that guardrail an actual edge instead of just an absence of jargon.

### WHY
*Why this matters; the values underneath the method.*

- The world deserves a new class of protagonists.
- Everyone deserves to visualize their impact on the world.
- Everyone deserves the responsibility of creating a world in their image.
- Everyone deserves autonomy over their lifestyle's design.
- We deserve an upgraded way of actualizing through this world.

**Ties to:** the assumptions-questioning messaging pillar (Section 2). This is the missing "so what": questioning your assumptions matters because autonomy and self-authorship require seeing your own blind spots first.

### WHERE
*Physically, emotionally, spiritually; the connection between who you are and the room you're in.*

- **Physically:** six chairs, two boards, one office space, one massive table.
- **Emotionally:** exposed.
- **Spiritually:** open.

**Ties to:** Section 6 (Physical Space Application) directly; this is a plainspoken description of the actual current room. Worth preserving that plainness rather than over-designing it away; the honesty is on-brand.

### HOW
*Not the plan or the strategy; the daily posture and rituals that build momentum.*

- Welcoming people and receiving data.
- Listening to every individual.
- A standing internal practice: identify what you're doing the most right now, then remove yourself from that task. If the resources aren't there to sustain that loop, solve it by becoming profitable, raising funds, or being resourceful; which naturally leads to team-building and delegation.

**Ties to:** this is currently internal operating philosophy, but it's specific enough to become a visible ritual (e.g., an actual welcome practice at the door, distinct from generic "onboarding"). Worth discussing whether to surface this to members directly or keep it as internal culture.

### WHEN
*When do you start.*

- Now. The "when" is always now.

**Ties to:** the adventurer/frontier urgency already present in the name and the Shackleton reference; a candidate line for signage or a closing statement in member-facing materials.

---

### A note on GAF and Tipper together

The same six questions were asked of both GAF and Tipper, and the parallel is itself informative for brand architecture: **same underlying belief, two different mediums.** GAF answers through presence; a room, a body, real exposure. Tipper answers through a system; a repeatable loop (listen, receive, reconstruct, present) encoded into software. GAF's "where" is a physical room; Tipper's "where" is a landing page. GAF's "who" is about being understood by people; Tipper's is about being understood by a system.

They are siblings under one philosophy, not one brand wearing two names. Design work for GAF should stay grounded in presence, room, and ritual; design work for Tipper should stay grounded in system, loop, and interface. Keeping that distinction explicit protects both brands from collapsing into each other.
